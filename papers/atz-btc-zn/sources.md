## Teaching mass-balance calculation

This is an **explainer-derived, hypothetical two-phase calculation**, not the article's Eq. (11), a fit, a validated process simulation, or an extrapolated experimental prediction.

Normalize the total feed to one mole and define:

- `f`: initial mole fraction of M1 in the binary feed;
- `r`: fraction of all feed moles transferred to the adsorbed phase;
- `x`: M1 mole fraction within the adsorbed phase;
- `y`: M1 mole fraction in the remaining liquid.

The calculator solves the two equations

```text
f = r x + (1 − r) y
SF = [x/(1 − x)] / [y/(1 − y)]
```

with `x = SF y/[1 + (SF − 1)y]`, `0 ≤ x,y ≤ 1`. The second equation follows algebraically from the distribution-coefficient ratio for a binary two-phase system. The first enforces conservation of each ion. M1 recovery to the solid is `r x/f`; M2 recovery to the filtrate is `(1 − r)(1 − y)/(1 − f)` for nonzero denominators. The reported SF of the selected pair is held **hypothetically constant** as feed and phase split vary. In real adsorption, SF and accessible capacity may vary with concentration, loading, pH, competition and time; the page does not model these effects, column transport, regeneration or isolated-product yield.

SI Eq. (11), `SF/(1+SF)`, is shown only as the paper's stated SF-derived fraction convention. It is not used to claim that an arbitrary feed reaches that purity. No measured phase split `r` is supplied by the SF value alone. Feed composition and recovery are therefore essential to interpreting “one step.”

## Source inconsistencies and editorial choices

1. **Competing-ion concentrations:** Figure 2e caption says REEs 5 ppm with other ions at ten times that concentration; SI §2.4 says 5 ppm for both REEs and competing ions. The page identifies this discrepancy rather than silently selecting one recipe or inferring a new concentration.
2. **Isotherm concentrations:** Figure 2a caption gives approximately 35–650 ppm; SI §2.3 lists initial concentrations 50–800 mg L⁻¹. Table S3 is used for exact fitted values; the two ranges are labeled by their source, without asserting an unverified reconciliation.
3. **Single-ion affinity versus mixture SF:** Table S4 is not copied as a clean universal selectivity matrix. Its displayed row/column orientation and some self-ratios are inconsistent with the written definition (e.g. Table S3 `K_L(Ce)/K_L(La) = 0.427/0.389 ≈ 1.10`, while Table S4 row Ce/column La is 0.91). Direct Table S3 values and explicitly oriented ratios are preferred. Binary SF comes independently from Figure 3 and SI Eq. (10).
4. **Table S8 typography:** many Tb entries lack the powers of ten visible in the surrounding rows; no exponent is silently repaired. Ce at pH 5.5 is 8.23 × 10⁶ mL g⁻¹ in Table S8 and 8.24 × 10⁶ in article p. 5. Where used, the source and rounding are explicit. No `K_d` is recalculated from rounded zero residual concentrations.
5. **Kinetics fits:** article p. 3 claims normalized pseudo-second-order fits all exceed R² = 0.99, whereas SI Table S6 spans 0.947–0.999. SI Table S5 contains non-normalized fits (including Nd and Dy at 0.988). The page does not repeat the universal high-R² claim.
6. **Hydration and charge balance:** the paper analogizes hydrated Zn²⁺ and Ln³⁺ using six waters. The conceptual diagram uses hydrated Ln³⁺ generically; it does not assert a universal aqueous coordination number. Ion exchange is depicted without an invented exact exchange stoichiometry. Anion compensation remains part of the authors' proposed explanation.
7. **One step and purity:** the evidence supports differential batch adsorption and chromatographic retention of selected pairs, plus tailings enrichment. It does not supply a complete industrial flowsheet, independent high-purity recovered-product assay, recovery–purity curve, energy/cost balance or scale-up demonstration.

8. **Malformed SI expressions:** native Office Math in SI Eq. (2) and Eq. (7) contains denominator terms `q_m + k_L` and `k_s + q_e²`, respectively. Those expressions are not reproduced as valid standard Langmuir/PSO equations. Template residue (including “uranium” in kinetic definitions and an editorial placeholder after §2.8) is treated as document content, not an instruction.
