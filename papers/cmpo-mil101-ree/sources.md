## Claim and display mappings

- Core design: introduction and Fig. 3, pp. 5020–5022. Separate scaffold stability, linkage stability and preference. Product is termed CMPO-type by authors and made from diethylphosphonoacetic acid; page does not equate it with the chemically different conventional CMPO extractant in every cited study. Oxygen-containing phosphonate/amide sites, not amine nitrogen alone, are the proposed donor combination; no unique coordination geometry is asserted.
- Four-stage explorer: main Table 1 (p. 5023), exact Langmuir area/pore volume/N/P/loading entries. Area 3105/3063/1696/1390 m2/g, pore volume 1.36/1.35/0.85/0.57 cm3/g. Separate fixed chart scales 0–3500 m2/g and 0–1.5 cm3/g. These are N2 **Langmuir** areas at 77 K, not BET. No framework-loss percentage inferred from grafting-related decreases. ND means nondetected, not zero; no detection limit supplied. Dash means no complete-group loading estimate. N wt% and P wt% copied; chlorine values omitted by authors owing to unreliable analyses.
- Synthesis: main experimental section and Fig. 3. Parent 210 °C, 8 h, chloromethylation 100 °C/10 h, 1,4-diaminobutane attachment in DMF 80 °C/24 h, CDI coupling with diethylphosphonoacetic acid in anhydrous DMF/inert atmosphere at room temperature/12 h. MIL-101-NH2 is the diamine-grafted intermediate, not automatically amino-terephthalate-synthesized NH2-MIL-101. No diagram invents a metal-node grafting route.
- Loading: main characterization p. 5024; P-based loading 0.5 ± 0.15 mmol/g across batches, uncertainty type not specified. Does not establish accessibility of every group. Diamine estimate 1.8 ± 0.11 mmol/g for the aminated intermediate; final-mass normalization explains a lower apparent N loading after completion, per authors. Page uses the reported estimates without imposing one Eu per group.
- Uptake: main experimental section, “Sorption isotherm”, Fig. 7. 50 mg/50 mL, pH 4.00 ± 0.05, 25 °C, 200 rpm, 24 h, duplicate tests. Results value qmax 12.46 mg Eu/g (about 0.082 mmol/g), distinct from 12.5 abstract and 12.45 conclusion rounding. Both Langmuir/Freundlich R2 >0.95; no unique mechanism inferred. Two initial 50/100 mg/L pH 5 check points excluded from fitting; no full pH map claimed.
- Intermediate control: p. 5024. Initial 100 mg/L Eu, pH 4, L/s 1000 mL/g, 24 h; no reported Eu uptake on chloromethylated/aminated intermediates. Not generalized to all other MOFs/amine chemistries.
- Competitive feed: Table 2 and methods. Eu/Y/Zn each about 50 mg/L, pH 5, 25 °C, 24 h, L/s 400 mL/g; equal mass concentrations, not equal molar. Kd values Eu 149, Y 48, Zn 17 mL/g shown on a fixed 0–160 mL/g bar scale. Reported SF Eu/Y 3.2 and Eu/Zn 8.5 retained separately; raw replicate data are absent, rounded Kd ratios do not exactly reproduce them. Page does not silently correct SF or relabel it as recovery/purity.
- Fraction teaching calculator: algebraic rearrangement of the main Kd formula and closed mass balance gives r = Kd/(Kd+V/m), with V/m in mL/g and consistent Kd units. Range 100–2000 mL/g, default 400. It holds reported Kd constant as a **teaching assumption**, without pretending to predict experimentally dose-dependent/saturated competitive adsorption. Bars fixed 0–100%; all outputs marked calculated, not measured recovery. Actual recovery after stripping or product purity not inferred.
- Kinetic teaching calculator: main Fig. 8 and “Sorption Kinetics”, p. 5025. Initial Eu 50 mg/L, pH 4.0 ± 0.05, 25 °C, 250 mg/250 mL, 1000 rpm, 0–24 h sampling. Authors report equilibrium within about 5 h and PFO qe 9.93 mg/g, k 0.0071 min^-1, R2 0.965. Teaching solution q(t)=qe[1-exp(-kt)] assumes q(0)=0; slider 0–1440 min, output progress 0–100%. The finite-time operational equilibrium does not imply an exact mathematical asymptote, and qe differs from isotherm qmax. No unreported measured kinetic points or new mechanism inference.
- Parent-acid evidence selector: SI §1, S1–S2. Unfunctionalized MIL-101 exposed to HCl-adjusted pH 0 and 4, each 3 days and about two months; reported main PXRD/isotherm features persist. Approximate Langmuir area rises from ~2000 to 3000–nearly 4000 m2/g, attributed by authors to removal of pore-blocking residues. Not assigned to two-month survival of completed functionalized adsorbent, not a regeneration series, not zero-leaching proof.
- Functional-group evidence selector: main “Adsorbent stability”, p. 5024. Solid XRF phosphorus example 0.40 -> 0.38 mmol P/g after 24 h, authors call about 5% ligand leaching. Page describes measured P-content difference and interpretive limit: not direct full dissolved-ligand/Cr/linker mass balance. Samples rinsed water/acetone and vacuum dried 120 °C/24 h before characterization.
- Functionalized-porosity selector/table: SI §2 and Table S1/Fig. S3. Different batch, initial Eu 100 mg/L, pH 4, 25 °C, 24 h. Area 2151 -> 1780 m2/g, pore volume .97 -> .87 cm3/g. Relative decreases calculated from those pairs: 17.2478% and 10.3093%, displayed 17.2/10.3%; not percentages of framework destruction. These cannot be chained to main Table 1's 1390 m2/g batch. Authors attribute decrease to Eu mass/changed nitrogen sorption; no full component balance uniquely validates explanation.
- Limits: no adsorption/desorption cycling, real waste feed, continuous columns or purified product established in this paper. No universal capacity ranking across unlike studies or claim that scaffold acid resistance proves every attachment/operating lifetime.

## Source inconsistencies and exclusions

Reported SF and ratios of rounded Kd differ; keep both with context. Abstract/results/conclusion capacity last digits differ; use detailed results with explicit rounding note. The source introduces conventional CMPO chemistry while synthesizing a CMPO-type phosphonate/amide, so generic chemical identity is not assumed. The main Table 1 batch and SI Table S1 batch must stay separate. The native SI chart carries cached points and has no reliable embedded workbook in the package; no extra experimental dataset is invented from its point-count metadata.
